# Multi-location brand: untangling the parent from its first location

A custom closet and wardrobe company with locations in several states ran everything through one website. Search engines kept confusing the parent brand with its original location, and the location pages, Google Business Profiles, and town pages overlapped.

## My role

A fixed-price, milestone-based engagement delivering recommendations and developer-ready structured data.

## What I delivered

### Entity clarification

A clear separation of the parent brand from the original location, so search engines and AI assistants understand which is the company and which is one of its showrooms. The structured data states the relationship explicitly, with the locations as branches of the parent organization.

### Home page and product schema

JSON-LD for the organization and its products, ready for the developer to install.

### Location pages tied to Google Business Profiles

Recommendations for location pages that match each Google Business Profile, so every profile links to a page about that specific location rather than the home page.

### Profiles versus town pages

Guidance for where a Google Business Profile and a local town page cover the same area, so they support each other instead of competing.

### Service listings and breadcrumbs

Recommendations for how services, regions, and locations should be organized and linked, with breadcrumb structured data reflecting it.

## Why it matters

Multi-location businesses lose visibility when every page and listing says the same thing. Clear entities, distinct location pages, and accurate structured data let each location rank for its own area and let the brand rank for itself.
