Your Google business reports are getting a new scoreboard

By Chris Abraham · Published October 24, 2026

If you run a local business, your Google Business Profile is the listing that shows your hours, your phone number, your reviews, and your spot on the map when a customer searches nearby. Google is now testing a rebuilt set of performance reports for that listing: five new sections that tell you far more than the old strip of numbers did. I wrote about the news on my weblog the day it broke. This guide is the owner version: what each section means, how to read the numbers without fooling yourself, and what to do about them each month.

What Google is changing

The old Performance tab was thin: a count of views, calls, direction requests, and website clicks, take it or leave it. The test version, reported by Search Engine Roundtable on October 23, 2026 from screenshots and video sent in by two practitioners who received it early, is a real reporting suite. It arrived one day after Google launched a new home portal for business profiles, which says this is a program, not a stray experiment. The reporter who has covered this beat longer than anyone called it a very big improvement on the current reports.

Calibrate before celebrating: it is a test. It may reach your account next week or next quarter, and Google has retired better ideas than this one. Nobody should rebuild a reporting habit this week on the strength of screenshots. But the direction is unmistakable. Google is investing in telling owners, in detail and with advice attached, what their listings are doing. An owner who learns to read the new scoreboard early gets the benefit of that investment first.

The five new sections, in plain language

Overview

The front page of the new reports, and the section to read first. At the top, Google places its own recommendations for your profile: the facts to complete, the photos to add, the details it thinks are costing you customers. Read it as a to do list written by the referee. The referee works for Google, so check the advice against your own judgment, but ignoring homework from the outfit that ranks you is rarely wise.

Engagement

What people did once your listing caught their eye: the calls placed, the messages sent, the bookings started, the website visits, the direction requests, gathered in one place instead of scattered. This is the section that comes closest to money, because each entry is a customer who moved from looking to acting.

Reviews

Your reviews, broken out in detail instead of flattened into a single star average. An average hides everything that matters. The detail view shows the recent reviews themselves, the patterns inside them, what customers praise, what they complain about, and whether each one got a reply. Reputation is most of the conversion game for a local business, and this section turns it from a feeling into a ledger.

Audience

Who is finding you. The audience section describes the people your listing reaches, including the split between people meeting your business for the first time and people coming back to it. Owners ask two questions more than any others: who is finding me, and is it the right crowd? This is the first time the profile reports have tried to answer the first one directly.

Search trends

The actual words people typed that brought your listing up, and how those words change over time. This is your market describing your business in its own language, which is rarely the language on your sign. If searchers keep finding you under words for work you no longer do, or fail to find you under the words for the work you want, this section shows it before the phone goes quiet.

Why your clicks and calls can fall while business holds up

Here is the reading skill the new reports demand, because the old habit will mislead you. A customer who asks Google a question increasingly gets the answer from an AI summary at the top of the results: your hours, your phone number, whether you are any good, read straight off your profile and your reviews. That customer got what they needed and never clicked anything. The action happened; it just never registered on your listing.

An analysis published in October 2026 followed three established florists in the United States and found profile interactions down 26 percent against the same period a year earlier, with website clicks down 30 percent, phone calls down 21 percent, and direction requests down 15 percent, on profiles that were active and well kept. Three florists are a signal, not a census, so read those numbers as direction rather than proof. The direction matches what I see across local accounts: the measurable actions on a profile are getting scarcer because the AI answers absorb them first. Your profile now matters as the source the machine reads, not only as the page the customer visits. Judge your marketing by the phone, the bookings, and the till alongside the report, never by one falling line on a chart.

Fifteen minutes a month: the owner routine

  1. Save the numbers first. Screenshot or export this month's figures before anything else. Baselines are the first casualty of every dashboard migration, and nobody can prove growth against a number that no longer exists.
  2. Read the Overview recommendations. Do each one, or hand it to whoever manages your listing with a date attached.
  3. Read every new review and answer each one. The new detail view is about to make neglected reviews visible to anyone who looks, starting with you.
  4. Compare against the same month last year. Seasonality makes last month a liar for most local businesses. The honest comparison is a year back.
  5. Read Search trends against your price list. The words bringing people to your door should match the work you actually want to sell.
  6. Ask the machines about your business. Once a month, put your name into Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity and note what the AI answers say. I keep a guide to measuring AI search for exactly this habit.

What to ask whoever manages your listing

  • Have the new reports reached my account yet, and if so, what do the Overview recommendations say?
  • What were my calls, bookings, and direction requests this month, set against the same month last year?
  • Has every review been answered, and what are reviewers praising and complaining about this month?
  • Which search words are bringing my listing up, and do they match the work I sell?
  • What do the AI answers say about my business this month, and is every word of it true?
  • What changed on the profile this month, and why?

A manager who can answer those six questions in writing, briefly, once a month, is earning the fee. A manager who sends a screenshot of a views chart is not. That standard is the whole of my Google Business Profile service: the work reported in writing, with numbers, on a rhythm an owner can hold me to.

When to worry, and when not to

Worry when the rating slides across several months rather than dipping once. Worry when reviews sit unanswered, when wrong facts surface in the AI answers about your hours, your address, or what you sell, and when Search trends drift toward work you do not offer. Worry most when every section falls at once and the phone goes quiet with them, because that pattern is a business problem and not a measurement problem.

Do not worry when clicks or calls ease downward while bookings and the register hold up: the machine answered on the spot, and the customer still came. Do not worry that your account lacks the new sections yet, since a test rolls out account by account. And do not worry over one soft month in a seasonal trade, which is weather, not climate.

Questions owners ask about the new reports

My calls are down. Is my business in trouble?

Not on that evidence alone. AI answers now hand out your phone number, your hours, and your rating before a customer ever reaches your listing, so some calls and clicks simply stop registering. Put the report next to your bookings and your till. If those hold while the chart falls, the likeliest story is that the answers absorbed the actions. If everything falls together, that is a real problem and worth a proper diagnosis.

Do I have the new reports yet?

Open your profile's Performance section and look for the five sections named in this guide. If they are not there, the test has not reached your account. Google rolls tests out gradually and quietly; even the trade reporter who broke the story did not have it on the day he published. Nothing is wrong with your account.

What should I do this week?

Three things. Save your current Performance numbers somewhere safe, because the old figures may not survive the migration. Answer any review that is still waiting for a reply. And check that the facts on your profile, hours, phone, address, services, are exactly right, because those facts are what the AI answers repeat.
