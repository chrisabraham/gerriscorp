# Starting a podcast to build your brand: what it really takes

By [Chris Abraham](https://gerriscorp.com/about/) · Published October 8, 2026

A podcast puts a founder's or an expert's actual voice in someone's ears for half an hour, by choice, while they drive or walk the dog. Few marketing channels earn that much attention. I recommend one to every client with something to say, along with a warning: the hard part is showing up, week after week, for a year.

## What a podcast does for a brand
- **Trust at scale.** Listeners hear how you think, how you handle a tangent, and what makes you laugh. By the time they call, they already know you.
- **A searchable record of expertise.** Every episode, with its notes and transcript, becomes evidence that you know your subject, for prospects, for search engines, and for the AI assistants people now ask about you.
- **Raw material.** One recording becomes show notes, an article, social clips, a newsletter item, and a transcript full of quotable lines.

## The real cost is commitment

A podcast rewards consistency long before it rewards polish. Pick a rhythm you can keep for a year, weekly or every other week, and protect it on the calendar like a client meeting. Many shows stop after a handful of episodes, so simply continuing puts you ahead of a crowd. Growth is slow, and the archive is the asset: the fiftieth episode makes the first forty-nine easier to find.

## A pocket recorder or a phone is enough

A small digital voice recorder, the kind Sony sells for less than a nice dinner, records clean audio, fits in a jacket pocket, and charges from a plain USB port. Buy two or three and keep one in every bag, so an idea never waits for the studio. A smartphone held close in a quiet room works just as well. A carpeted bedroom beats an echoing kitchen with an expensive microphone every time.

Noise used to be the reason people bought gear. Adobe Podcast's Enhance Speech, which has a free version, now takes a noisy recording and brings it close to studio quality in a minute or two. Audacity, free on every computer, handles the edit: trim the false starts and the long pauses, and leave the personality in.

## Hosting and distribution are free

A podcast host stores the audio and publishes the RSS feed that every app reads. Spotify for Creators, formerly Anchor, hosts shows free, publishes them on Spotify, and gives you the feed to list on Apple Podcasts and every other directory. Record on whatever you carry, upload the file, write the notes, schedule it, and every app picks it up.

## YouTube is a separate channel

YouTube can import an audio podcast from its RSS feed, but it turns each episode into a video over a still image, and those videos live only on YouTube and YouTube Music. YouTube audiences expect faces, cuts, and visuals, and they discover shows through YouTube's own recommendations. Treat it as its own channel with its own effort, and keep publishing the audio feed everywhere else.

## Spend money on the parts that repeat

An intro, an outro, and the show's main cover art appear on every episode, so they repay a professional. Freelancers on Fiverr produce all three for a modest fee. For individual episodes, ChatGPT turns a title and a mood into a custom cover in minutes, and a distinct cover per episode makes each one easier to spot in a feed and easier to share.

## Let AI clear away the chores
- **Cleanup:** AI enhancement rescues recordings made in imperfect rooms.
- **Transcripts:** hosts and apps generate them automatically. Publish them, because they make every episode searchable.
- **Show notes:** paste the transcript into an assistant, ask for a summary, chapter points, and a title, then edit for your voice.
- **Cover art:** an image model gives every episode its own look.
- **New formats:** Google's NotebookLM turns notes or an episode into a two-host audio discussion, an easy bonus episode for listeners who enjoy that style.

Your opinions stay yours. AI removes the busywork that makes people skip a week, and skipped weeks are what end shows.

## Own a home for it

Your host's pages belong to the host. Give the show its own website on its own domain, with a page for every episode, and the archive works for your brand in search for years.
