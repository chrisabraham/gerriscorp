# Fact-checked writing in your voice

AI drafting is fast and cheap. The risk is what it adds: a date that's off by a year, a cause of death nobody reported, a quote smoothed into something the speaker never said, a motive assigned to you that you never stated. I write with AI and then fact-check the draft line by line, the way a magazine checks a feature before it goes to print.

## Who this is for

Founders, experts, and executives who have more to say than time to write it; shows and channels that want articles grown out of episodes; organizations whose writing has to survive a skeptical reader. Every piece goes out under a real name, so every claim in it has to hold.

## How a piece gets made
1. **Material first.** A recording, a transcript, an interview, your notes, a thread, your photos. The draft is built from what you gave me, and nothing is invented to fill a gap. Gaps come back to you as questions.
2. **Voice rules.** Your words, your banned words, your habits, your sense of humor, and the constructions you can't stand, written down once and applied to every draft. AI prose has recognizable tics, and the rules strip them.
3. **Draft and revision.** I draft with AI, revise by hand, and send it to you to read.
4. **The check.** Every claim is traced before publication, described below.
5. **Publication.** A search-ready title and description, structured data, images with alt text, and links to the pages the piece supports.

## What the check covers
- Names, titles, dates, ages, places, and figures, confirmed against primary or reputable sources and cited in the piece.
- Your own statements, compared with the recording so the piece matches what you actually said.
- Quotations, copied exactly and attributed to the person who wrote or said them, including commenters and posters.
- Metrics, labeled for what they count. Views, upvotes, members, and visitors are four different numbers.
- Feelings and motives, kept only when you stated them.

With the finished piece you get a short list of what the check changed, why, and the source for each change. Anything I can't confirm is cut or flagged for your decision.

## Why it matters for search

Google's spam policies target content produced at scale to manipulate rankings and content that misleads readers, whatever tool wrote it. Accurate pages with named sources are the ones AI assistants are most willing to quote.
