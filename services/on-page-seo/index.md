# On-page SEO

Every page should tell people, search engines, and AI assistants exactly what it's about and why it deserves the click. On-page SEO is how a page says that.

On-page SEO covers everything on the page itself: the title, the meta description, the headings, the copy, the internal links, the images, and the structured data. It's where technical SEO meets writing, and it's where I spend a lot of my time, because a technically perfect site still fails if its pages are vague.

## What I work on

### Titles and meta descriptions

A title tag that names the topic like a news headline, usually 50 to 60 characters, and a description of 140 to 160 characters that tells a searcher what they'll find. I avoid pipes, dashes, and repeating the brand on every page, and I write each one for the page it's on. Google rewrites titles and descriptions it finds unhelpful; well-written ones are rewritten far less often.

### Headings and structure

One clear H1, then H2 and H3 headings in a logical order that a reader, a screen reader, and a crawler can all follow. Headings that carry information, so each section can stand on its own when an AI system quotes it.

### Copy that matches intent

A service page that plainly explains what you do, for whom, and how. An explanatory article that answers its question completely. I follow legitimate keyword priorities, and I write for the person searching first, because that's what both Google and AI assistants reward.

### Internal linking

Descriptive links between related pages, so authority flows to the pages that matter and every important page sits within a few clicks of the home page. Orphaned pages, the ones nothing links to, get found and connected.

### Images

Descriptive file names, alt text that serves people using screen readers, explicit dimensions to prevent layout shift, and modern formats at sensible sizes.

### Structured data

Schema.org JSON-LD for your organization, people, services, products, articles, FAQs, and breadcrumbs, consistent with what the page visibly says. FAQ and HowTo rich results are now rare in Google, but structured data still helps search engines and AI systems understand who you are and what each page covers.

## Publishing, too
