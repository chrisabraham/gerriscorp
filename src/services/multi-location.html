Multi-location and connected businesses

A parent brand with several locations. Sister companies sharing an owner, a building, or a phone number. Stores in neighboring towns competing with each other in Google. These businesses confuse search engines and AI assistants unless their identities are untangled deliberately.

Common problems

  • Search engines can't tell the parent brand from its original location, so the wrong one shows up.
  • Location pages that repeat the same copy with a city name swapped in.
  • Google Business Profiles that link to the home page instead of the matching location page.
  • Sister businesses with conflicting names, addresses, or phone numbers across the web, or duplicate listings.
  • Structured data that describes every location as the same business, or several businesses as one.
  • Several related sites and domains, some redirecting, some not, competing for the same searches.

What I do

Entity architecture

A clear map of who is who: the parent organization, each location, each sister business, and how they relate. Then structured data that states it, using organization and local business types with parent and branch relationships, so search engines and AI systems describe each one correctly.

Location pages that earn their place

One page per real location, with the address, hours, services, staff, directions, and local details that make it useful, linked from the matching Google Business Profile. Service-area pages where the business travels to customers, written for that area instead of cloned.

Consistency everywhere

Names, addresses, phone numbers, and categories aligned across websites, profiles, and directories, with duplicates found and resolved.

Connected businesses kept distinct

Separate content, FAQs, and structured data for each business, even when they share an owner and an office. A plumbing company and a foundation repair company with the same owner need different answers to different questions.

Multi-site cleanup

Old domains, duplicate sites, and inconsistent domain versions consolidated with 301 redirects, and Search Console history preserved for each property.

Seasonal planning

For seasonal businesses, risky changes such as category edits that can trigger reverification happen in the off-season, and a change freeze covers the peak.

How it starts

A paid diagnostic maps every site, profile, and listing the business controls, finds the conflicts, and ranks the fixes. Implementation follows in the order that protects existing rankings, visibility, and reviews first.

Common questions

Should each location have its own website?

Usually one site with a strong page per location works best, because the locations share the brand's authority. Separate sites make sense for genuinely separate businesses.

Can you fix suspended or duplicate profiles too?

Yes, as part of the same engagement. See Google Business Profile hard cases.
