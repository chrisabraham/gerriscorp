When a Customer Asks for the Best, a Machine Answers

Somebody in your service area is, right now, asking Google a question like "best [your trade] near me" or "who should I call for [your service]." A year ago they got a list of businesses and made their own shortlist. Today they get a written answer: two or three names, a sentence about each, and a recommendation. If your name is missing from that paragraph, the shortlist was made without you.

The shift is already measured. Search Engine Land, reporting Semrush's tracking of 400 US desktop commercial keywords, found that Google's AI Overviews appeared for 88% of them in August 2026, nearly double the share in September 2025. Review searches, exactly the "best," "top-rated," and "near me" questions above, moved the furthest. The searches that end in a phone call are the searches the machines took over first.

What the answer says about a business nobody is tending

An AI answer is only as good as the record it is built from: your website, your Google Business Profile, your listings, and your reviews, reconciled by a system that cannot call you to check. When that record is untended, the answer still arrives, and it arrives wrong in predictable ways:

  • It names a competitor whose facts happen to agree with each other across sources, because agreement is what the system reads as truth.
  • It describes the business you used to be, services you dropped, a location you closed, hours you changed, because old listings never die on their own.
  • It blends you with somebody else: a similarly named business across town or across the country, your details and theirs averaged into a company that does not exist.
  • It leaves you out entirely on the searches that carry the most money, while happily citing you for trivia.

Each of those is what an untended record produces, automatically, every day.

Why service businesses sit in the blast radius

Review and recommendation searches are buying searches. The person asking has a problem, a budget, and a short afternoon. For many of them, the machine's paragraph is the whole of their research. A business can have loyal customers, strong reviews, and a good website, and still lose that customer before the first click, because the answer was assembled from a profile nobody has opened in three years and a directory listing nobody claimed.

The good news is that the same mechanism cuts the other way. The answer rewards the business whose record is consistent, complete, and claimed. That is work. It is also, for once, work a smaller business can finish before a bigger competitor bothers to start.

Check it yourself in ten minutes

Pick the three searches that bring you the most work, such as "best roofer in Arlington" or "emergency plumber near me," and run each one in Google, ChatGPT, and Perplexity. Write down whether you're named, how you're described, and which source each answer cites. Then open your Google Business Profile and compare its name, hours, services, and service area with your website. Every mismatch you find is a fact the machines can read two ways. Google's own guidance says there are no special tricks for AI Overviews: a page has to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet, and the rest is ordinary, accurate SEO.
